[SYNERGY]
A Matchmaking + Execution Platform for Early-Stage Startups
Full Feature Breakdown

1. The One-Sentence Pitch
An app that looks at what your startup has and what it needs, finds the other startup or mentor who complements you — not just someone similar — and then gives you both a shared workspace to actually get the work done, instead of the connection dying in a DM.

2. The Problem, In Detail
Founders need a lot of things to grow: beta testers, co-marketing partners, mentors, technical advice, industry connections, warm intros. Today, all of that runs through the same broken pipeline:
Discovery depends entirely on your network. If you don't already know the right person, you're stuck. This especially disadvantages founders who are newer, less connected, from smaller cities, or outside existing "founder circles" — often the exact people who need the help most.
Founders don't know how to describe what they need. Someone might type "we need help getting our product in front of people" when what they actually need is beta testers, or a co-marketing partner, or a distribution mentor — three very different kinds of help that need three different search strategies.
Manual search is exhausting and low-yield. Scrolling LinkedIn, cold-messaging strangers, posting in Slack groups and hoping — this eats founder time with a low hit rate.
Even a good match usually goes nowhere. Two people connect, have one good call, say "let's stay in touch," and then... nothing. There's no shared to-do list, no deadline, no record of who promised what, and no consequence for ghosting.
There's no accountability or reputation system. If someone flakes on a deal, there's no record of it anywhere. The next founder who reaches out to that same person has no idea.
The app is designed to intervene at every one of these failure points, not just one.

3. Who Uses the App (User Types)
Startups
Founders or team members representing an early-stage company. They can act as either side of a match — offering resources (e.g., an active audience, technical skill, a testing ground) and/or requesting resources (e.g., beta testers, marketing help, expert guidance).
Mentors / Domain Experts
Individuals with specific expertise — business strategy, product development, marketing, fundraising, technical architecture, industry-specific knowledge — who want to offer guidance to startups, typically in a lighter-weight, advisory capacity rather than a full working partnership.
(Platform Admin / Moderator — internal role)
Reviews new tags submitted during onboarding before they join the shared vocabulary, handles disputes that can't be resolved through the mutual-confirmation flow, and monitors for abuse or fake profiles.

4. Onboarding Flow, Step by Step
Sign-up & verification. New users verify via a business email domain or a roster check (e.g., confirming affiliation with a startup accelerator, university program, or known founder community), so the platform stays a trusted space rather than an open marketplace.
Choose your role. Startup, mentor, or both (a founder can also mentor others in their area of strength).
AI-assisted profile builder. Instead of filling out a rigid form from scratch, the user describes themselves in their own words — e.g., "We're a 6-person team building a budgeting app for students, about 8 months in, and we're looking for people to actually try it out and tell us what's broken." An AI assistant reads this and:
Extracts what the startup offers (e.g., "active development team," "student-focused product")
Extracts what the startup needs (e.g., "beta testers," "early user feedback")
Suggests standardized tags from a shared, controlled vocabulary (so "people to try our product" and "early adopters" both map to the same underlying tag: Beta Testers)
Flags anything that doesn't match an existing tag for admin review, so the vocabulary grows in a controlled way instead of fragmenting into a thousand slightly different tags
Structured details. After the AI pass, the user confirms or edits: industry, stage (idea / pre-seed / seed / growth), team size, audience size (if relevant), and specific goals for using the platform.
Mentor-specific fields (if applicable). Areas of expertise, past experience, availability (e.g., one-off calls vs. ongoing check-ins), and what kind of startups they're most excited to help.
Profile goes live. The user starts receiving and browsing matches immediately.

5. The Matching Engine, In Detail
What it looks for
The engine doesn't search for people who look like you — it searches for people who complete you. A startup with "strong audience, weak technical skills" is a good complement to a startup with "strong technical skills, no audience yet." A founder who needs fundraising guidance is better matched with a mentor who's raised capital than with another early-stage founder who hasn't.
The five scoring factors
Factor
What it measures
Example
Need Fit
Overlap between one side's needs and the other's offerings
Startup A needs "beta testers"; Startup B has "500 active app users willing to test new features"
Expertise Fit
(Mentor matches only) Whether the mentor's specialty addresses the founder's actual goal
Founder's goal: "pricing strategy"; Mentor's specialty: "SaaS pricing and monetization"
Industry Fit
Whether the two are in the same or adjacent industries
A fintech startup and a fintech-focused mentor score higher than a totally unrelated pairing
Stage/Size Fit
Whether the two are at a compatible stage or size
A pre-seed startup and a Series-B mentor might still work for advice, but two startups with a 10x audience gap would be down-weighted for an audience swap specifically, since the trade wouldn't be equal
Goal Fit
Whether both sides want the same kind of outcome from the engagement
One side wants a one-time favor; the other is looking for an ongoing partnership — this mismatch lowers the score even if the tags overlap

How matches are scored and shown
Each factor is weighted and combined into a single percentage-style compatibility score. Every match includes a plain-language explanation, for example:
85% Match — Strong need/offer overlap (beta testing), same growth stage, moderate industry overlap, clear goal alignment.
This means users never see a mysterious ranked list — they always know why someone was suggested, which builds trust in the system and helps them decide who to reach out to first.
Match discovery experience
A daily/weekly digest of new high-scoring matches, so users don't have to constantly check the app.
A browse/search view where users can look through all potential matches, filter by type (startup vs. mentor), industry, or engagement type.
A "why this match" expandable detail on every suggestion, showing the score breakdown.
The ability to save/bookmark a match for later without committing yet.

6. From Match to Deal Room
Match suggested. The app surfaces a potential partner or mentor with a score and explanation.
Interest expressed. Either side can express interest; when both sides do, a Deal Room is created automatically. (Optionally, a lightweight introductory message can be sent before both sides fully commit, similar to a "like" before a "match.")
Deal Room created from a template. Based on the type of engagement (Beta Testing Exchange, Co-Marketing Swap, Mentorship Engagement, etc.), the Deal Room is pre-populated with the right milestone checklist, relevant fields, and a dedicated chat thread.
Terms Agreed stage. Both sides discuss and lock in specifics — scope, timeline, expectations — before moving to active work. This stage exists specifically to prevent the classic "we never actually agreed on what we were doing" problem.
In Progress stage. Work happens. Milestones get checked off one at a time as they're genuinely completed.
Launched stage. The core deliverable of the engagement is complete (e.g., the beta test has run, the newsletter swap has gone out, the mentorship goal has been addressed).
Reviewed stage. Both sides submit blind ratings and short feedback; the engagement is archived but remains visible in both parties' histories.

7. Deal Room Features, In Detail
Milestone checklist — specific to the engagement type, editable only within reason (major changes require both sides to re-agree).
Mutual confirmation — a milestone isn't marked complete until both sides check it off. This prevents one party from claiming progress that didn't actually happen.
Evidence attachments — for milestones that matter most (marked with an asterisk in the templates below), users can attach a screenshot, document, or link as proof. This creates a lightweight audit trail without turning the app into a heavyweight project management tool.
Dedicated chat — scoped to that specific deal, so conversations don't get lost across unrelated threads.
Deadline/timeline field — optional target dates per milestone, with reminders.
Status badge — visible at a glance: Matched, Terms Agreed, In Progress, Launched, or Reviewed.
Dispute flag — if the two sides disagree about whether a milestone was actually completed, either party can flag it for a lightweight admin review rather than letting the deal silently stall.
Archive & history — completed Deal Rooms stay accessible as a record, both for reference and because they feed each user's trust history.

8. Trust, Ratings & Safety Features
Blind, simultaneous ratings. After a deal is marked "Reviewed," both sides submit a rating and short written feedback without seeing the other's response first. This prevents the common problem on other platforms where people rate generously just to avoid retaliation or to get a nice rating back.
Trust history. Every user builds a visible track record: number of completed deals, average rating, and how often they follow through versus how often deals stall out. This becomes a genuinely useful signal for deciding who to engage with.
Verification badges. Confirmed via business email or program roster, shown on every profile.
Reciprocity-inflation safeguards. Because ratings are blind and simultaneous, and because the platform can track patterns (e.g., a user who always rates everyone 5 stars regardless of outcome), ratings stay meaningful over time.
Reporting & flagging. Users can report inappropriate behavior, ghosting, or bad-faith engagement, feeding into admin review.

9. Types of Engagements, In Detail
The platform uses a template system — each engagement type is a reusable configuration (fields + milestone checklist + evidence requirements), not custom-built code. This means new deal types can be added over time without rebuilding the app.
Beta Testing Exchange (Startup ↔ Startup)
For startups that need real users to test their product and give feedback.
Scope defined → Testers assigned → Testing round* → Feedback submitted* → Issues reviewed
Good fit when: one side has an active user base and the other has a product ready for outside testing.
Newsletter / Audience Co-Marketing Swap (Startup ↔ Startup)
For startups that want to cross-promote to each other's audiences.
Assets shared → Draft approved → Send date locked → Swap executed → Results reported*
Includes an audience-size compatibility check, since a wildly uneven trade (e.g., 50,000 subscribers vs. 500) isn't a fair swap — this gets reflected directly in the match score.
Mentorship Engagement (Startup ↔ Mentor)
For founders who want guidance rather than a working partnership.
Goals defined → Session(s) scheduled → Guidance delivered* → Follow-up actions logged → Engagement reviewed
Flexible structure: can be a single one-off call or an ongoing relationship with recurring check-ins.
Future engagement types (not built yet, but supported by the architecture)
Warm Intro Facilitation — one side connects the other to a valuable contact.
Technical Collaboration — shared technical work, e.g., API integration testing.
Investor Prep / Pitch Review — a mentor reviews a pitch deck or does a mock investor Q&A.
Each of these could be added later simply by defining a new template — no core rebuild required.

10. Where AI Shows Up, In Detail
1. Onboarding Assistant
Turns casual, unstructured self-descriptions into clean profile data.
Suggests tags from the existing controlled vocabulary first, before proposing a brand-new tag.
Flags ambiguous input back to the user for clarification (e.g., "Did you mean you need funding advice or investor introductions? These are matched differently.")
New tags go through a lightweight admin review before becoming part of the shared vocabulary, keeping the tag system from becoming cluttered or inconsistent.
2. Smarter Matching Layer (added later, once there's real usage)
Understands near-matches that don't share exact wording — e.g., recognizing "early adopters to try our product" and "beta testers" refer to the same underlying need.
Deliberately introduced after the core rule-based system is stable, so early users always get transparent, explainable matches first, and the "smart" layer only adds precision on top rather than replacing the explainable logic entirely.
Where AI does not replace human judgment
Final decisions to connect, agree to terms, or complete a deal are always made by the humans involved — AI only assists with describing needs and surfacing better-ranked suggestions.

11. Notifications & Everyday Use
New match alerts — when a new high-scoring match appears.
Deal Room activity — messages, milestone updates, and confirmations needed.
Reminders — for pending confirmations, upcoming target dates, or a deal that's gone quiet.
Rating prompts — once a deal reaches "Launched," a nudge to move it to "Reviewed" and leave feedback.
Digest mode — a daily or weekly summary option for users who don't want constant pings.

12. Example User Journey (End to End)
Maria runs a 4-person budgeting app startup, 8 months in, with almost no active users yet.
During onboarding, she types a casual description of her situation. The AI assistant tags her as needing Beta Testers and offering an active dev team and a completed MVP.
The next day, she gets a match: another startup, Campus Grocer, that has 600 active student users and is looking to offer testing opportunities in exchange for early access to new tools. Score: 82% — strong need fit, same stage, overlapping student-focused audience.
Maria expresses interest; Campus Grocer's founder does too. A Beta Testing Exchange Deal Room is created automatically.
They agree on scope in the "Terms Agreed" stage: 50 testers, a 2-week testing window, feedback via a shared form.
As the test runs, both sides check off milestones together — "Testers assigned" gets mutually confirmed, "Testing round" gets a proof attachment (a screenshot of testing activity), and so on.
Once feedback is submitted and reviewed, the deal moves to "Reviewed." Both founders leave blind ratings — Maria rates the engagement highly, and so does Campus Grocer's founder.
Maria's trust history now shows one completed, well-rated Beta Testing Exchange — making her a more attractive match for the next founder who considers working with her.

13. What Makes This App Genuinely Different
Most comparable platforms do only one half of this:
Some are good at suggesting connections, but leave users to "take it from here" with no structure — so promising matches quietly die.
Others offer solid project-tracking tools, but have no smart way of deciding who should be paired up in the first place — they assume you already found the right person.
This app is built to own both halves of the problem end to end: it finds the right complementary partner using a transparent, explainable score, and then gives that match a real structure — milestones, mutual confirmation, evidence, and trust-building ratings — to make sure the connection actually turns into finished work.

14. Quick Recap
Core idea: Match startups with what they're missing — not who they resemble — then help them actually finish the collaboration.
Key features: AI-assisted onboarding, explainable multi-factor match scoring, two match types (startup-to-startup and startup-to-mentor), templated Deal Rooms with milestone tracking, mutual confirmation, evidence attachments, blind post-engagement ratings, and a visible trust history.
Supported deal types today: Beta Testing Exchange, Co-Marketing Swap, Mentorship Engagement — with an architecture that supports adding more later.
Why it works: It replaces "who do I happen to know" with an active, explainable system for finding the right fit, and replaces "let's stay in touch" with a real, trackable plan to get something done.
